# ECON 5560 replication: Shah et al. (2019)

Individual replication repository for ECON S5560 (Cal Poly, Fall 2026), Liam Hyde. It reruns my team's (Team 1) replication from the raw data to the final tables and figure with one R command.

## Paper

Shah, P., Dissanayake, S. T. M., Fujita, Y., and Nunes, P. A. L. D. (2019). Impact of a local, coastal community based management regime when defining marine protected areas: Empirical results from a study in Okinawa, Japan. *PLOS ONE*, 14(3), e0213354. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0213354

The paper is a split-sample choice experiment with Okinawa residents. Half saw a nationally managed (top-down) marine protected area, half a community-managed (bottom-up) one. It fits mixed logit models (Table 3) and turns them into willingness to pay (WTP, Table 4). The headline result is that WTP for the protected-area attributes is higher under community management.

## Data

Both files in `data/raw/` are exactly as obtained; the first script checks their MD5 hashes before anything else runs.

- `data_impact_community_based_mgt_on_MPAs.dta`: the authors' replication data, Harvard Dataverse, https://doi.org/10.7910/DVN/OELWEF (CC0). Downloaded 2026-09-25. It has no variable recording which alternative each respondent chose, so the paper's models cannot be estimated from it alone.
- `MPA_journal_data_with_choice_for_CalPolyFA2026.xlsx`: the same data with the observed choice added (`Choice`, `Alt`), prepared for this course and received by our team on 2026-10-26. It is not publicly posted elsewhere. The first script checks that its 19 shared columns are identical to the Dataverse file.

Neither file is over 50 MB (1.2 MB and 0.6 MB).

## How to run

Requires R 4.1 or newer with the `mlogit`, `dfidx`, `haven` and `readxl` packages (`install.packages(c("mlogit", "dfidx", "haven", "readxl"))`). From the repo root:

```
Rscript run_all.R > logs/run_log.txt 2>&1
```

`run_all.R` runs the four numbered scripts in `code/` in order and stops at the first error. Its last line is `print(sessionInfo())`.

## Files

- `run_all.R`: master script; runs every step below in order, then prints `sessionInfo()`.
- `code/01_check_data.R`: checks file hashes, panel structure, and that the two data files agree; writes `outputs/data_checks.csv`.
- `code/02_table2_descriptives.R`: reproduces Table 2 (respondent characteristics by survey version); writes `outputs/table2_descriptives.csv` and `outputs/table2_income_sensitivity.csv`.
- `code/03_mixed_logit_tables3_4.R`: estimates Models 1 and 2 (Table 3) with `mlogit` and WTP (Table 4) next to the published values; writes `outputs/table3_coefficients.csv`, `outputs/table4_wtp.csv` and `outputs/model_summary.csv`.
- `code/04_figure1_wtp.R`: draws attribute WTP by management regime, ours beside the paper's; writes `outputs/figure1_wtp.png` and `.pdf`.
- `data/raw/`: the two raw data files described above.
- `outputs/`: everything the scripts produce; regenerated by `run_all.R`.
- `logs/run_log.txt`: full output of the clean run described below.

## What did and did not reproduce

Table 2 reproduces: all ten group means match the paper to two decimals. The age standard deviation matches the population SD (9.78 and 9.36); the sample SD is 9.79 and 9.37, and the paper does not say which it used. Income is a category code 1 to 6, and code 6 means "don't know / no answer"; the paper's means only come out if code 6 is kept as a number, so I keep it and report the mean without it in `table2_income_sensitivity.csv`.

The sample is 824 respondents (422 national, 402 community), not the paper's 827. The posted data are short three community respondents, and I could not find them.

Table 3 mostly reproduces. Every main-effect attribute and payment coefficient in both models is within 0.0004 of the published value, with the same significance level. The ASC (the value of any protected area over the status quo) is smaller: 7.61 vs 8.22 in Model 1 and 7.55 vs 8.46 in Model 2, though still highly significant. Three of the five community interactions match the paper in sign and significance: fish × community (0.0084, not significant; paper 0.0076, not significant), coral × community (0.0138, 5%; paper 0.0168, 5%) and coastal × community (0.0093, 10%; paper 0.0105, 10%). Payment × community has the paper's sign but is smaller and not significant (−0.0004; paper −0.0011, 5%). ASC × community is 0.98 and significant at 5% (paper 0.225, not significant). The log-likelihoods are −3126.41 (Model 1) and −3119.21 (Model 2).

Table 4 mostly reproduces. Attribute WTPs are within 14% of the paper, and six of the nine are within 5%. WTP is higher under community management for all three attributes, as the paper finds, and the same WTPs are significant (national fish catch and coastal protection are not). ASC WTP is 1,361 yen pooled (paper 1,489), 1,406 national (paper 1,632) and 1,476 community (paper 1,374), so community comes out above national, the reverse of the paper's order.

Table 5 (the marginal rate of substitution between natural and man-made capital) is not replicated here.

The ASC differences should be read with care. The ASC's standard deviation is large (about 8.4 to 8.8), so the simulated likelihood is flat in that direction and the ASC terms depend on the draws. These results match my team's mlogit run exactly, and the team's draw check (500 to 2000 Halton draws and three pseudo-random seeds) found the ASC terms moving by about as much as their gap from the paper: Model 2 ASC × community ranged from −0.01 to 0.98, and community ASC WTP fell below national in three of the six settings. The paper does not report its software or number of draws, so I could not match those.
